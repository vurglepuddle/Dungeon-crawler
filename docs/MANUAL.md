# UNDERCROFT

**LOST MOTH • 1993**  
*A Finder's handbook. Please return it when found.*

## A word from the abbey

Things do not disappear. This is the first rule of lost property.

They slip behind the world: a cook's knife, a mason's stone, the warm coat
somebody still remembers. Under the abbey they settle in rooms older than
the abbey itself. There they wait, and the waiting changes them.

You are a Finder. You have six measures of health, a rust knife, eight pockets,
and the sort of judgement that brought you here in the first place.

The Dawn Seal lies on the deepest floor. Bring it back to the entrance above.
The monks will call that success. You may find another good deed on the way.

## The three watches

The Abbey Below keeps the familiar stone of the brothers' cellars. Beneath
it lie the Drowned Courses, where the mortar remembers every winter's rain.
The Lower Watch is deeper still: old guard stones, soot, iron and pale ash.
The sentries have long since misplaced their relief. They have kept the watch.

There the Husk holds the main passage, and a Hound keeps an interest in the
key loop. A corded chapel and a webbed storeroom reward the curious. Gather
light, food and fresh padding before committing yourself. A discarded coat
may be a better discovery than a handsome weapon.

The deeper inhabitants strike harder. A careless meeting with a deep Husk
or Wick Ghost takes twice the old toll; a Hound's rush may take half a
Finder's strength. Their warnings have not grown shorter. Read the tell,
guard or leave the threatened ground, and answer during recovery. An intact
coat still takes the whole first blow. The Lower Watch admires caution;
it has already seen a great many demonstrations of courage.

## Before descending

Choose **NEW RUN**, then enter a seed or choose **RANDOM**. **START** replaces
the previous expedition; **CANCEL** leaves it alone. Seeds are at most six
characters. The entry notice excludes I, O, 0 and 1: the abbey has suffered
enough arguments about handwriting. Try **BELL42** when comparing maps with
another Finder. The same seed and floor count give the same starting dungeon.

**CONTINUE** resumes a saved expedition. Your journey is recorded as you act
and when the game is paused or closed. If an **!** replaces the floor display,
touch it to inspect the save problem and try again. Check it before leaving.
An older expedition keeps the cellars it began with. CONTINUE does not add
a new watch beneath your boots while you are away.

At the end, **RETRY SEED** starts that expedition afresh. **NEW RUN** lets you
choose another. Death carries no possessions into the next attempt.

## The six movement stones

The six arrow buttons beneath the view are arranged thus:

| Upper left | Upper middle | Upper right |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turn left | Step forward | Turn right |
| Step sideways left | Step backward | Step sideways right |

Curved arrows turn you a quarter turn. Straight arrows move one square;
sideways steps preserve your facing. Turning is free. Moving spends a beat.
There is no need to hurry while deciding.

Touch **MAP**, or the compass, to examine your explored route. The map does
not reveal unseen rooms. Opening it, looking around, examining a creature,
and choosing a possession spend no beat. **WAIT** spends one. An action the
game refuses spends none.

Touch a usable object in the picture; its small gold glint helps you find it.
The named verbs below the view tell you what your next touch will do. If
several possessions lie together, a list asks which one you want. The abbey
does not expect you to identify a knife by its single brightest pixel.

Stairways, lifts and the entrance can be recognised before they can be
reached. If touching one says **Step closer.**, move nearer and try again;
that distant touch spends no beat. At a reachable travel face you may touch
it or step forward into it. A dead lift needs its voice restored, and the
entrance still expects the Seal. If someone occupies the far stair landing,
**SIDLE** offers a clear neighbouring square when one is available.

## What is in your pockets?

Touch a pack icon to read its card. **WIELD** puts a weapon in your hand;
**WEAR** puts a coat on your body. The old equipment returns to the pocket
the new piece vacated. Touch equipped gear for its card; **PACK** puts it
away if there is room.

**EAT** uses bread or a bone for one health, or red tonic for two, up to your
six-health limit. Yes, tonic uses the EAT button. Our compositor has been
informed. At full health, nothing is consumed. A padded coat absorbs one
blow; once spent, taking it off does not sew the stuffing back in.

**DROP** leaves a pack or equipped item on the square beneath you. It stays there.
**CLOSE** dismisses the card and clears your selection. Touching your own
portrait also clears the selection and recalls the arrow instructions.

Eight pockets fill quickly. Use, equip, or drop something when they do.
Keys and the Dawn Seal have their own safe belt: a full pack cannot prevent
you taking them, and you cannot accidentally eat or discard them.

Item cards show capabilities such as **HEAVY**, **EDGE**, **FOOD**, and **GLOW**.
These are working descriptions. Weight holds a plate; an edge parts a cord;
food may settle a hungry creature as readily as it settles you. Light is
also a tool, though it pays to notice what is hiding from it. Your view
remains lamplit without a fuel clock; there is no need to race the lamp.

## Borrowing a little fire

Cold reeds will not burn a web through force of intention. Carry a reed
torch to a burning brazier and touch the brazier: **LIGHT** kindles the
reeds. They may be selected, in your hand, or waiting in a pocket. You keep
the same torch; walking past the fire does not light it by accident.

With lit reeds in your hand, or selected in your pack, touch a web to
**BURN** it. The torch remains yours and stays lit. A knife's **CUT** deals
with cord. Neither discovery demands that you throw away your tool.

**TOSS** remains an alternative. Select a suitable possession and choose
TOSS to examine its landing lane before committing; **CANCEL** leaves it
with you. A thrown lit torch can burn a web, and a thrown morsel may reach
a hungry Hound. What the dungeon does not consume stays where it lands.

## A gate is a question

Your own weight presses a floor plate. Step away and its gate closes. To
hold it open, **DROP** something HEAVY while standing on the plate, or select
a HEAVY pack item and **PLACE** it on the plate directly ahead.

Beyond the gate is a latch. Use **LATCH** to keep the route open, then recover
your weight if you wish. A body or heavy object in the doorway also holds a
gate: these gates do not crush things. A keyed gate needs its matching belt
key and **UNLOCK**. The open gate stays open.

## Those waiting below

**The Bell Husk.** A hollow bell that has learned to walk. It approaches
through a clear lane, raises an arm, and announces where that arm will land
next beat. Turn to inspect it without penalty. **GUARD** catches the blow;
stepping backward lets it strike the square you left. Then **HIT** during
its recovery. The knife does one damage; an ordinary HEAVY counter does two.
Striking a raised, braced arm does only one. Read the arm before trusting
the size of your weapon. Even the starting knife can win without a scratch.

**The Bell Porter.** Its silence is a loss, not a threat. Its missing clapper
waits in a side room. Touch the Porter to hear its request. **RETURN** the
clapper from hand or pack and both lift landings wake; **RIDE** carries you
between the entrance and the deepest floor's entry landing. Ordinary stairs
still work in both directions. There is no deadline for kindness.

You may keep the clapper. It is a HEAVY weapon with a three-damage counter,
and **RING** can interrupt Husks in clear sight ahead, within three squares
and a quarter-circle of your facing. They reel for a beat: take your chance.
The ring works once per floor. Putting the clapper down, changing floors,
or reopening the game does not recharge a floor already rung. An empty call
costs nothing. A used clapper can still be returned.

**The Ash Hound.** Its name is less important than the lane it has chosen.
Watch the warning before its rush. Leave that lane or GUARD; then make use
of its recovery. FOOD offers another answer. Select a morsel and **FEED**
it when close, or use a clear TOSS lane. A settled Hound is still a body:
leave it room, and do not mistake its good manners for an empty square.

**The Wick Ghost.** Some lights are cold enough to cast a shadow of their
own. Beyond a corded passage, bring a GLOW possession within reach and
**DISPEL** it. A lamp answers the Ghost; a shard can part the cord that
keeps you apart. The Dawn Seal has a light the old stone remembers.
Inspect the creature and the possession rather
than assuming every bright thing behaves alike. Ordinary fighting remains
an answer for a Finder who has mislaid the more elegant one.

## The lost-property tradition

A Finder reads the little histories. Something useful to you may be the
very thing its owner has waited centuries to hear again.

Keep the clapper, or restore the lift: both routes can bring the Seal home.
Restoration is a choice with a consequence, not a toll disguised as charity.
The floors remember open gates, creatures, equipment, and things you leave
behind. Absent floors wait while you are elsewhere; stairs and lift arrivals
do not grant a creature an extra arrival blow.

On reaching the deepest floor, take the Dawn Seal. Return to the first floor's
entrance and choose **LEAVE**. Merely finding the Seal is only half the story.

## Graph paper department

Draw one square per step. Mark north before drawing the first corridor; a
sideways step changes your square, not your compass heading. Label stairs
with the floor they join, and circle a latched gate. Use a different pencil
mark for a plate still relying on a stone. A useful map remembers why a way
is open as well as where it goes. The automap will help, but paper never
forgets where you intended to return.

## Customer services, cellar extension

The title screen keeps separate **SFX ON/OFF** and **MUSIC ON/OFF** switches.
They remember your preference. Quiet expeditions are perfectly respectable;
the written warnings remain available when the household is asleep.
There is no score penalty for consulting a map or pausing to think.
